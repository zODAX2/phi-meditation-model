Φ Simulator

A controllable threshold model of a self-sustaining absorbed state. Set the drives a practitioner trains, shape the region of the body that is held, light a spark, and watch whether the state catches, holds at its ceiling, survives a release, and switches off on demand.

∂Φ/∂t = (α·I − β)·Φ + κ·Φ² − γ·Φ³ + D·∇²Φ + s + η

Φ is a latent variable: the intensity of the absorbed state at each point of a two-dimensional body map, not a physical field. s is a steady drive (a generated spark such as goodwill or recalled joy) and η is spontaneous fluctuation (a noticed spark). γ is the ceiling, fixed at 1 unless the ceiling is removed; the map's edges let nothing in or out; all units are dimensionless.

Reading the results

Held
Φ settles at the seed level inside the region and stays low outside: the stored, self-holding state.
No spark
The state formed without ignition: a steady drive lifted the region past its tipping point, or fluctuations grew where rest was unstable.
Drained
Φ went back to rest: the region was too narrow or the wrong shape, the spark too small, or β too high for a seed level to exist.
Spreading
The state crossed the region's edge and kept spreading across the map, because unattended regions barely decay.
Runaway
With the ceiling removed (γ = 0), nothing caps the state, so once above threshold Φ grows without limit.

Why it is controllable

Inside the window −κ²/4γ < αI − β < 0, rest and the seed state both exist. Entry needs a spark above the threshold, γ caps the state at a plateau, and exit is a deliberate move of the gain below −κ²/4γ. Between those, the same settings can hold either rest or the seed, depending on history: that is what the release test in the protocol measures.

This page shows the mathematics of the model and nothing more. It cannot show that a real practitioner's state follows these dynamics. The accompanying protocol tests that, with heart-rate measures, an active control and stop rules fixed in advance.
