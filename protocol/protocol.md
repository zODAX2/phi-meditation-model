# The Φ practice: a step-by-step protocol

Companion to *Φ: An Equation Simulation for Meditation Practice*. Version 1.0, 15 October 2026. Licence CC BY 4.0.

This is the practice written as you do it: the daily drills, the full session step by step, a comparison method, the control session, the programme, the fixes, the safety rules and the log. Every step trains or measures one term of the equation.

The goal of a session is a **seed**: a pleasant, whole-body state that reaches a plateau, carries your attention instead of being carried by it, and survives three minutes with no driving at all. In the model, that is Φ crossing its threshold and the loop κ holding it.

The practice has three phases, and each gates the next:

1. **Daily drills.** Train noise, coupling and shape separately, about 10 minutes each, until each reaches Level 2.
2. **Full sessions.** The eight-step session, 45 to 60 minutes, alongside control and comparison sessions.
3. **Deepening.** Fade effort, respect the ceiling, and run the blocks of the programme.

One rule runs through all of it: **direct, effortful focus collapses the state; wide, interested, lighter attention sustains it.** Wanting the result is the main obstacle, so treat each session as if it does not count, and log how much you wanted it.

**Attention only.** No forced breathing and no breath-holding at any step. The breath is counted or felt, never steered.

## Before you begin

Everything here is done once, before the first drill. Write the criteria and the stop rules on paper first, so a session that merely felt good cannot be counted afterwards.

**Equipment**

- [ ] A chest strap that exports raw beat-to-beat intervals, with software that computes RMSSD
- [ ] Two interval timers: one chiming every 30 seconds (release test), one every 5 minutes (half-effort checks)
- [ ] A voice recorder running for the whole session, for saying "now" at onset and the ratings aloud
- [ ] A breathing pacer set to 6 breaths a minute, for control sessions only
- [ ] Printed front-view body outlines on a grid, and a pencil
- [ ] One chair or cushion and one posture for every session, seed and control alike: posture alone changes heart-rate variability
- [ ] A fixed session time relative to waking; for shift workers, sessions only after sleep and never between 03:00 and 06:00
- [ ] Level 0 baseline: two weeks of the 2-minute gate recording, to learn your resting RMSSD and its spread
- [ ] For comparison blocks: one intention sentence, written down and kept unchanged for the whole block
- [ ] For Block C only, optional: a consumer EEG headset

**The four seed criteria**

1. **Onset.** You marked the moment the pleasant state began carrying itself, by saying "now" into the recorder.
2. **Strength and size.** It reached 7/10 or more, and the body map drawn afterwards covers at least half of the head and torso.
3. **Self-holding.** It stayed at 6 or more through two half-effort checks, five minutes apart.
4. **Storage.** At the end of the 3-minute release test, the rating is still at least half the plateau, and RMSSD is still above its pre-session value.

A session meeting criteria 1 to 3 but not 4 is logged as *held by effort*: real progress, not yet a seed.

**Before Block A:** preregister the predictions, RMSSD as the primary marker, the analysis and the stopping rule (for example on OSF).

## Phase 1 · Daily drills

Train each variable alone before full sessions combine them. Modules 1 to 3 run together from the start, about 10 minutes each, at a different time of day from your session. Pass a level on three separate days before moving up. Full sessions begin once all three reach Level 2.

### Module 1 · Noise (β): breath counting

β drains every other term, so it is trained first and longest. Count the breath; never pace it.

1. Sit in your session posture, eyes closed. Breathe naturally.
2. Feel the breath at the rim of the nostrils or the upper lip, where air touches skin.
3. At the end of each out-breath, count silently: one. Next out-breath: two. Up to nine, then start again at one.
4. Each time you lose count or find yourself past nine, add a tally mark and restart at one. Note the lapse lightly ("thinking"), soften for a moment, and return. Each return is the repetition you are training.

| Level | Drill, daily | Pass to move up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Count | 10 min, eyes closed | 3 errors or fewer, on 3 separate days |
| 2. Lengthen | The same for 20 min | 4 errors or fewer in 20 min, on 3 days |
| 3. Under noise | 10 min, eyes open, recorded noise from a busy place playing | 3 errors or fewer, on 3 days |
| 4. Fast settle | Sit straight after 5 min of mental arithmetic; record 2 min of HRV from minute 3 | RMSSD at or above your Level 0 median, in 4 of 5 attempts |

### Module 2 · Coupling (α): receive and send at the heart

α has two halves: receiving the body's signal and sending into it. The heart comes first because the strap can check both. Keep the body still while sending: relaxing muscles also lowers heart rate, and this is a test of attention.

**Receive.** Rest attention at the centre of the chest and listen for individual beats without touching your pulse. Count them silently in a window, then compare with the strap.

**Send.** Send one clear, simple signal inward, such as "slow" or "soften", then stop sending and listen again for what changed. Keep the breath natural: if the heart slows only when the breath slows, that is the breathing route, not attention, so log which it was.

| Level | Drill, daily | Pass to move up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Count with feedback | Count heartbeats in windows of 25, 35 and 45 s; check each against the strap immediately | Accuracy 0.85 or higher, on 3 days |
| 2. Count blind | The same, checking only after all three windows | 0.85 or higher, on 3 days |
| 3. Send with feedback | Watching live heart rate, send "slow" for 60 s with natural breath, rest 60 s; 5 rounds | Average drop of 5 bpm or more against rest, on 3 days |
| 4. Send with the breath held steady | Breathe to a metronome at your resting rate so the breath can't carry the change; same rounds, no screen | Average drop of 3 bpm or more at the same breath rate, on 3 days |

Accuracy = 1 − |actual − counted| ÷ actual, averaged over the three windows. After every drill, also rate how distinct heart, gut and head felt, 0 to 10: that is α's resolution, logged without a pass mark.

### Module 3 · Richness (I) and shape against spread (D): the sphere

I rises when more regions are bound into one state. D cannot be trained; width, roundness and fast re-widening are how you beat it.

1. Feel the space your head occupies: the whole volume inside it, not its surface.
2. Add the chest. Ask: *can I feel the space my head and chest occupy at the same time?* Let both be there at once.
3. Add the belly, down to the pelvis.
4. Let the three round out into one shape: a sphere centred behind the breastbone, large enough to hold the head above and the belly below.
5. If attention collapses to one spot, don't drag it back. Ask the question again and let the other regions return. Tally each collapse.

It is working when the volume feels like one object and holding it costs less than holding three places. Common errors: spreading into the limbs and the room too early, which thins the field; holding three separate places instead of one volume.

| Level | Drill, daily | Pass to move up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Two at once | Head and chest felt at the same time, 2 min | 2 collapses or fewer, on 3 days |
| 2. One sphere | Head, chest and belly as one sphere, 5 min, then draw the body map | 3 collapses or fewer and one connected shaded area, on 3 days |
| 3. Re-widen | Narrow on purpose to one point for 10 s, then re-widen; 10 rounds | Back to the full sphere within 5 s in 8 of 10 rounds, on 3 days |
| 4. Eyes open, moving | The sphere for 5 min with eyes open, then 2 min of slow walking | 3 collapses or fewer, a connected map, roundness at least as high as eyes-closed |

**The body map.** On a gridded body outline, shade every square where the state was felt. Area = shaded squares. Roundness = 4π × area ÷ perimeter², where the perimeter counts shaded-square edges touching an unshaded square. Compare maps with each other, never with a perfect circle.

## Phase 2 · The full session

A full session runs 45 to 60 minutes in eight steps:

1. Gate (3 min)
2. Settle and count (15–25 min)
3. Find the spark (2–5 min)
4. Feed the loop (5–15 min)
5. Spread it (about 5 min)
6. Hold and check (5–10 min)
7. Release test (3 min, fixed)
8. Off switch and log (3 min)

If no spark grows within 15 minutes of step 4, go back to counting for 5 minutes and try once more. If it still doesn't grow, run the release test anyway and log the session as it was.

### Step 1 · Gate (3 min)

The gate tells you whether a seed is possible today. Above your ceiling, the model says nothing will catch.

1. Put on the strap (wet the electrodes first), sit in your session posture, and start the recording. Leave it running for the whole session.
2. Sit for two minutes with eyes closed and the breath left completely alone. One deliberate slow breath raises the reading and spoils the gate.
3. Rate your mental noise 0 to 10 *before* you look at any number: 0 is quiet, 10 is racing. Then note the 2-minute RMSSD.
4. Rate how much you want a seed today, 0 to 10.
5. Log noise, desire, RMSSD, hours since waking, and anything unusual about sleep or work.

During Block A, every session runs in full whatever the gate says: those weeks set your ceiling. After Block A, a session above the ceiling runs as β-only: steps 2, 7 and 8.

### Step 2 · Settle and count (15–25 min)

**Settle (2–3 min).** This clears surface noise by subtraction; it adds nothing. Eyes closed, let your weight drop into whatever supports you, and feel each contact point: seat, back, feet, hands. Travel once, slowly, from the top of the head down, letting held effort go at each place: eyes, jaw, tongue, shoulders, belly, hands. Leave the breath alone. When a thought arrives, notice it and return to the contact points; don't follow it and don't fight it.

**Posture.** Upright without leaning into anything you could sag into, chin slightly down, hands resting where they don't pull on the shoulders. Eyes closed, or resting half-open on the floor.

**Count.**

1. Feel the breath where air touches skin: the rim of the nostrils or the upper lip.
2. Breathe naturally. Never steer the breath.
3. At the end of each out-breath, count silently, one to nine, then start again.
4. Lose count or reach twelve: the mind wandered. Note it lightly, soften the body, restart at one.
5. After several rounds without a lapse, drop the numbers and simply feel each breath from beginning to end.

**Signs you have reached access.** Attention stays with the breath without being pulled away. Thoughts are faint and in the background. The breath often becomes very fine; don't take a deep breath to check. Stay here 5 to 15 minutes before moving on. Moving on too early is the most common reason the spark never grows.

| What happens | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Dull, sleepy, foggy | Open the eyes slightly, straighten up, count more precisely; if it persists, a minute standing |
| Restless or agitated | Let the count land on the out-breath, soften belly and jaw, widen to the whole body for a few breaths, then return to the spot |
| Straining, pressure in the head | Too much effort. Hold the spot the way you'd hold a small bird: firmly enough that it stays, gently enough not to crush it |

### Step 3 · Find the spark (2–5 min)

There are two ways in. Keep one way for a whole block of sessions, so the log can tell them apart.

**Noticing a pleasant sensation (the default; η in the model).**

1. From access, let attention leave the breath and look for anything mildly pleasant already present: warmth or tingling in the hands, the face, the centre of the chest, or the smoothness of the breath itself.
2. Try a small, genuine half-smile and feel what happens around the eyes and cheeks.
3. Mild is enough. You are looking for the faintest sense of "this is nice".
4. If several places feel pleasant, choose the one easiest to stay with.
5. If nothing pleasant shows up within about two minutes, return to the breath for five more minutes: concentration isn't deep enough yet.

**Generating it (s in the model).** Use this when noticing doesn't work, and as the generated route in Block C.

1. **Goodwill.** Bring to mind someone easy to love. See their face. Offer three short wishes silently and slowly: may you be happy, may you be safe, may you be at ease.
2. **Or joy.** Recall a specific moment of real gladness and replay it in detail.
3. Notice where the warmth or lift lands in the body, usually the chest or the face.
4. Let the person, the wishes or the memory go, and stay with the bodily feeling itself. The image was the match; the feeling is the flame.

### Step 4 · Feed the loop (κ) (5–15 min)

This is the engine. Attention makes the pleasant feeling stronger, and the stronger feeling holds attention. Your only job is to let that loop run.

1. Rest attention on the *pleasantness* of the sensation: not on where it is, how strong it is, or whether it's growing.
2. Do nothing else. Don't name it, comment on it, analyse it or try to help it along. Every "is this it?" is a small push, and pushes break the loop.
3. Let it change however it wants. If it moves or turns from warmth into tingling or lightness, follow the pleasantness, not the old location.
4. Typically it grows a little, wavers, grows again, and then takes off: it starts holding your attention instead of you holding it. That is onset. Say "now" once, quietly, into the recorder, and go straight back to the pleasantness.
5. If it fades, concentration dipped. Go back to the breath for 3 to 5 minutes, then return.

**Wanting is the main obstacle.** Strong desire for the result creates the effort that blocks it. When you notice wanting, treat it as one more sensation, let the body soften, and return to the pleasantness.

**Keep the hand light.** Hold the pleasantness the way you take in a whole room, not the way you stare at one object in it.

**What onset is not.** A brief rush or tingle that comes and goes is common even in light concentration. It counts as onset only if the state then carries your attention for minutes.

### Step 5 · Spread it (I and shape) (about 5 min)

1. Keep resting in the pleasantness, then open attention around it: first the area right next to it, then the whole chest, then the whole torso.
2. Add the head, then the arms and legs, until you are feeling the whole body at once.
3. Let it round out into a sphere centred in the chest, as trained in Module 3.
4. Don't chase the edges or force pleasantness into places it isn't. You are widening attention; the pleasantness fills what it fills.
5. If attention collapses back onto the original spot, rest there a moment, then open again.

Teachers disagree here: some let a local spark spread, others stay on a single point. Block B tests it (H1).

### Step 6 · Hold, check and stop at the plateau (κ and γ) (5–10 min)

**The half-effort check, at every 5-minute chime:**

1. Deliberately drop about half of whatever effort you're making, for 30 seconds. Attention stays where it is, with a lighter hand.
2. At the end of the 30 seconds, say the rating 0 to 10 aloud.
3. If it holds at 6 or above, κ is carrying it. Stay at that lighter effort.
4. If it collapses, effort was holding it. Go back to step 4 and feed the loop again, rather than pushing harder.

**The plateau.** At some point the state stops getting stronger. Don't try to make it bigger: that is the runaway the ceiling γ exists to stop, and it shows up as agitation, pressure in the head or a headache. Let it become calmer and steadier instead. Note the minute it levelled off and its rating.

### Step 7 · Release test (3 min, fixed)

This is the decisive measurement. A stored seed persists without driving; an effort-held state falls away.

1. Start the 30-second chime. The strap keeps recording.
2. Open your eyes and rest them on one neutral point at eye height.
3. Stop all driving: no attention to pleasantness, no widening, no checking. Don't hold the state and don't push it away. Just sit.
4. At each chime, say the rating 0 to 10 aloud: six ratings in all.

Common errors: quietly holding it, which is driving; deliberately dropping it, which is the off switch. The skill is the middle way, and control sessions are where you learn it.

### Step 8 · Off switch and log (3 min)

1. Switch the state off on purpose: stand up, move, shake out your arms, walk a few steps, and name five things in the room aloud.
2. Check that it has ended. If something is still running, move and talk more.
3. Draw the body map: shade where the state was at its plateau, darker where it was stronger.
4. Fill in one row of the session log: what happened, not what it meant.

The off switch ends every session. A state that will not switch off is a stop-rule sign, not a success.

## The comparison method: the conviction route

This route runs in comparison sessions in Block B, alternating with the pleasure route, so the data can decide which builds a seed faster. A comparison session uses steps 1 and 2 above, then the steps below in place of steps 3 to 6, then the release test and off switch as usual, logged as *Conviction route*.

**A. Open wide (2–3 min).** Build the sphere exactly as in Module 3.

**B. Conviction hold (6–10 min).** Hold the sphere. Bring in the intention sentence, not as words repeated but as a stance: the felt sense of meaning it completely. Let it matter. When doubt or distraction comes, don't argue with it; return to the stance. Common errors: repeating the sentence mechanically, straining, expecting a particular result.

**C. Clean pass (6–10 min).** Drop the intention completely, all at once. Observe plainly what is present: where, what quality, how strong. Compare with the hold: what remained, what vanished? Fix one to three plain observations to log. Common error: keeping a softer version of the conviction running.

**D. Train the coupling (6–10 min).** For each centre in turn, heart, then gut, then head, about 3 minutes each: receive for 60–90 s; send one simple signal ("slow", "soften") for 30–60 s, then stop; listen again and note yes, no or unclear; repeat once. Last, notice whether the three centres felt distinct or blurred.

**E. Ignite (1–2 min).** Return to the whole sphere in one move. Turn everything fully on at once for 30 to 60 seconds: the full conviction, all three centres live together, the whole sphere held. Think "fully on", not "push harder". The breath stays natural. Then stop pushing, stay wide, and notice whether it catches. Say "now" if it does. Common errors: building slowly; narrowing to the strongest spot at the peak; using the breath to force it.

**F. Let it feed itself (5–10 min).** Rest attention on the state's most present quality. Let interest, not effort, keep attention there. About once a minute, check: *is effort carrying the state, or is the state carrying my attention?* If it is effort, lower the effort a notch. When it stops growing, stay at that level. Rate the plateau.

## Phase 3 · Deepening

### Module 4 · The spark and the loop

Opens once Modules 1 to 3 are at Level 2.

| Level | Drill, daily | Pass to move up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Find it | After 10 min of counting, find a mildly pleasant sensation within 2 min and stay with its pleasantness for 1 min | Found in 3 of 3 attempts, on 3 days |
| 2. Feed it | Stay with the pleasantness only, doing nothing else, for 5 min | Rating rises, or holds at 4 or more, on 3 days |
| 3. Generate it | 2 min of goodwill or recalled joy, then 3 min with the bodily feeling alone | Rating of 5 or more at the end, on 3 days |
| 4. Onset | Steps 3 and 4 in full, inside a session | A marked onset holding at 6 or more for 2 min, in 3 of 5 sessions |

### Module 5 · Self-feedback (κ): fading the effort

Opens after your first marked onset.

| Level | Drill, daily | Pass to move up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Who carries it? | After onset, 3 min resting on the state's quality; each minute note "effort" or "state" | "State" on 2 of 3 checks, on 3 days |
| 2. Fade the effort | Lower effort one notch a minute for 4 min, from full to none, rating at each step | Rating at zero effort at least half the starting rating, on 3 days |
| 3. Short release | 60 s with eyes open and no driving | Rating at 60 s at least 70% of the plateau, on 3 days |
| 4. Full release | The 3-minute release test in full sessions | Persistence beats the control by more than 2 SD in 3 sessions |

### Module 6 · The ceiling (γ): hold, don't climb

| Level | Drill | Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Find the plateau | In step 6, rate every minute and mark the first minute it stops rising | Name the plateau minute in 3 sessions, later ratings within 1 point of it |
| 2. Hold, don't climb | Stay at the plateau without pushing for more | 10 sessions in a row with no push past the plateau and every stop rule clear |

A plateau that keeps rising session after session, or a state that runs on for hours, means the ceiling is failing: stop ignition and widening, keep only settling, and read the stop rules.

### The control session: paced breathing

Runs on alternate days, as long as a seed session, and is built to be able to beat you.

1. Gate, exactly as step 1.
2. Breathe with the pacer at 6 breaths a minute, 5 seconds in through the nose and 5 seconds out, eyes closed, for as long as steps 2 to 6 took in your last seed session.
3. Release test (step 7): the pacer stops, eyes open, a rating every 30 seconds.
4. Off switch and log (step 8), with the type set to *Control*.

### The programme

1. **Level 0 (2 weeks).** Baseline gate recordings; Modules 1 to 3 daily.
2. **Gate 1:** Modules 1 to 3 at Level 2.
3. **Block A (weeks 3–6).** Seed sessions alternating with control sessions, every session run in full to set your ceiling. Module 4 opens.
4. **Gate 2:** a marked onset meeting criteria 1 to 3 in 3 of 5 sessions.
5. **Block B (weeks 7–12).** Pleasure route against conviction route in alternating blocks; spread-versus-point sessions. Module 5 opens.
6. **Gate 3:** release-test persistence beats the control by more than 2 SD in 3 sessions.
7. **Block C (weeks 13–18).** The generated-spark route, the early-warning test, and the optional EEG detector.

### The early-warning test (H7)

If onset is a tipping point, the body's signals should slow down and swing more widely in the minutes before it. The strap carries the test, so you never have to rate during the build-up.

1. Keep the strap recording through every seed session and say "now" at onset; the recorder's timestamp marks the moment.
2. After the session, export the beat-to-beat intervals.
3. Take the 5 minutes before each marked onset. Cut them into 60-second windows, a new one starting every 10 seconds, and compute in each the variance of the intervals and their lag-1 autocorrelation.
4. Do the same for matched stretches from sessions where step 4 ran but no onset came.
5. The prediction holds if both measures rise toward onset more than in the matched stretches. Fix the exact test in the preregistration before Block C.

### Optional: a blind EEG detector (Block C)

Record a consumer EEG headset in every seed and control session of Block C, with identical posture and eyes closed in both. Label minutes from marked onset to release as *seed* (only in sessions meeting criteria 1 to 3) and the matching minutes of control sessions as *control*. Train a simple classifier (logistic regression on band power and signal complexity in 10-second windows) on the first two-thirds of the days and test it only on the remaining days, with the pass mark fixed in advance. Treat it as exploratory: consumer headsets pick up muscle and eye movement easily.

## When it doesn't work

| What happens | Most likely term | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No pleasant sensation anywhere | β: access not reached | Back to counting for 5 to 10 more minutes; stay longer in access next time |
| The sensation appears, then vanishes | β: concentration dipped | 3 to 5 minutes on the breath, then return |
| It grows a little, then stalls | κ blocked by effort or commentary | Stop helping. Rest on pleasantness only; let each "is this it?" go |
| Intense rush, then a crash | γ: pushing past the plateau | Ease off and let it calm. Aim for steady, not strong |
| Pressure or brightness pulling up into the head | The field narrowing upward | Let attention drop to the chest and widen through the torso |
| It holds only while I keep trying | κ near zero: held by effort | Longer in step 4 with smaller effort drops; compare with the control |
| Strong in session, gone in the first 30 s of release | κ ≈ 0 | Let effort fall for longer before the release |
| Sleepy, sinking, pleasant fog | Dullness passing for calm | Straighten up, open the eyes a little, count precisely |
| Wanting it so much it never comes | Desire acting as noise (β) | Practise as if this session doesn't count; log desire 0 to 10 |
| Nothing ever catches (conviction route) | β above ceiling, or spark too small | Check the gate; ignite the whole sphere at once, never a point |
| The centres blur together | α resolution low | Lengthen the receive phase at each centre |
| Catches only on days you felt sure | Expectation painting the result | Lengthen the clean pass; trust the strap over the ratings |
| The plateau keeps rising, or the state runs on for hours | γ failing | Stop ignition and widening; settling only; read the stop rules |
| It only works in long sessions | Dose | Plan 3 to 5 consecutive days with several sessions a day |
| Nothing after a night without sleep | β from sleep loss | Sessions only after sleep |

## Safety and stop rules

Meditation is not risk-free. A systematic review found adverse events in 8.3% of participants, most often anxiety and depression (Farias et al., 2020). Absorption practice adds its own: energetic body sensations, disturbed sleep, heightened sensitivity, and frustration or craving when access comes and goes.

**Stop and get an outside read if any of these appear:**

- more than two nights in a row of disturbed sleep
- panic, or feeling unreal or detached for more than a day
- intrusive traumatic memories
- thoughts of harming yourself
- involuntary movements or energy surges that continue outside sessions
- a state that will not switch off with the off switch

**Always:**

- Attention only: no forceful breathing and no breath-holding at any step.
- Never practise while driving, at height or in water.
- The off switch ends every session.
- Stay at the plateau; never push past it.
- With a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder, practise only with clinical oversight.

This trains attention, interoception and steadiness. It is not medical treatment and doesn't replace it.
